Research Interests

Timing-aware state estimation and uncertainty-aware locomotion control.

My deployment experience has made me interested in how RL or MPC controllers should respond when observations, actions, and low-level updates do not arrive on the nominal schedule.

Question

What should a controller believe is happening now?

I am interested in systems where sensor data, issued actions, and physical actuation refer to slightly different moments. One possible direction is to estimate a short-horizon current-state distribution from timestamped history, then give an RL or MPC controller both the estimate and a calibrated confidence signal. I would like to investigate whether this can improve graceful degradation as delay, jitter, or missed cycles grow.

Experimental grounding

Hardware experience behind the research question.

The proposed direction is grounded in two instrumented real-robot stacks rather than presented as an isolated concept.

Timing conditions

Four ways a nominal loop can become a different loop.

  1. 01Observation delay

    The controller acts on a state whose contact or body motion may already have changed.

  2. 02Action delay

    A command reaches the plant later than the policy or optimizer assumes.

  3. 03Jitter

    Variable compute or transport timing makes a single fixed-delay model incomplete.

  4. 04Missed cycles

    Held or skipped updates alter the effective action history.

Possible method

Estimate, calibrate, then adapt the controller's response.

This diagram describes a possible research direction. Interfaces and evaluation criteria would need to be established on instrumented simulation and hardware loops.

Evaluation ideas

Measure both control outcomes and the quality of uncertainty.

I would begin with controlled timing faults in simulation and then move to an instrumented wheel-legged or quadruped platform when the setup is ready.

Perturbations

Observation delay, action delay, variable jitter, bursty missed cycles, and combinations with friction or actuator mismatch.

Baselines

Domain-randomized PPO, recurrent policy, deterministic state predictor, fixed-delay compensation, and a controller without confidence input.

Control outcomes

Task completion or fall, recovery time, tracking error, intervention threshold, and degradation as timing faults increase.

Estimation outcomes

Prediction error, interval coverage, calibration error, out-of-distribution detection, and added compute latency.

Ablations

Remove timestamps, action history, missed-cycle indicators, or confidence-conditioned control to test which inputs matter.

Continued research

Directions I hope to continue exploring.

These directions connect my deployment experience with research on adaptation, optimization, failure, and hybrid locomotion.

01

Failure-adaptive control

I am interested in whether timing uncertainty can be a useful signal for controllers that adapt as physical failure becomes more likely.

02

Fast adaptation and control

I hope to explore how fast, physically structured adaptation could use a timing-aware state estimate without violating real-time constraints.

03

Real-time optimization

My systems work motivates a concrete question: how should compute budget and worst-case latency enter the controller design itself?

04

Hybrid locomotion

Wheel-legged motion combines contact transitions, continuous rolling, and changing traction, which may be a useful setting for timing-aware control studies.

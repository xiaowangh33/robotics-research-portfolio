THE DEPLOYMENT PROBLEM

Stable in simulation did not mean recoverable after a real impact.

The first hardware policy could stand and move, but an external kick produced growing oscillation and eventual loss of balance. The useful question was not “does PPO work?” It was which unmodeled effect—contact variation, motor response, gains, body properties, or control timing—was pushing the real closed loop outside the trained distribution.

I broadened dynamics randomization and disturbance exposure, then redeployed through the same verified interface. The retained training configuration includes randomized mass, center of mass, friction, actuator gains, reset state, persistent force/torque offsets, and impulse-like pushes. Action and proprioceptive latency are randomized over zero to five policy steps.

The result shown above is qualitative hardware evidence of kick recovery. No unrecorded success rate or robustness percentage is claimed.

One control loop, five timing domains.

The deployed path separates policy inference from the higher-rate transport and motor loops. Frequencies below are taken from the archived configuration and source comments.

TIMING AS A SAFETY-RELEVANT SIGNAL

The code already needed an operational definition of “current.”

Two microcontrollers cannot be merged safely just because both packets arrived. The Linux bridge pairs feedback from the same observation_seq epoch, checks local sample age and USB arrival skew, and stops rather than combining incompatible state.

EPOCH CONSISTENCY

Same observation sequence

Feedback from the two devices is accepted as one robot observation only when it belongs to the same logical epoch.

FRESHNESS BOUNDS

Age and skew checks

The archived bridge uses a 10 ms local-age bound and a 30 ms USB arrival-skew bound for paired feedback.

FAIL-CLOSED

No common state, no continued motion

If a common observation cannot be formed for 500 ms, the system latches a stop. Per-motor feedback also has its own stale-data handling.

SOURCE-TRACEABLE EVIDENCE

Claims that can be followed from model contract to motor transport.

TRAINING CONFIGURATION

Latency is sampled as complete delayed packets.

Archived action and proprioception terms retain a short FIFO and select a delayed policy step, preserving a coherent vector instead of independently corrupting scalar entries.

DEPLOYMENT MODEL

Exported tensor dimensions are explicit.

The ONNX contract is obs[1,57] → actions[1,16]; the deployment state machine constructs the same observation ordering used by training.

COMMUNICATION LAYER

Four CAN buses distribute 16 actuators.

The design documentation estimates bus utilization per channel and uses dual STM32F407 controllers rather than hiding transport behind a single nominal “real-time” label.

HARDWARE MEDIA

Nominal locomotion and recovery are separately documented.

Outdoor locomotion and short disturbance tests are retained as raw project videos, allowing behavior to be reviewed without an invented aggregate metric.

OUTDOOR DEPLOYMENT

Locomotion beyond the training scene.

The outdoor video records the platform on real pavement and grass. It is included as direct evidence, not as a claim of exhaustive terrain generalization.
